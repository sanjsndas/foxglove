About Foxglove Sunday Supper

An independent recipe journal for slow cooking, seasonal eating, and the quiet art of feeding people well.

Foxglove Sunday Supper began as a personal recipe box — a place to keep the dishes that kept showing up on our table. Over time, it grew into a small journal for anyone who finds comfort in the rhythm of a slow kitchen.

We are independent and reader-supported. No sponsored content, no pop-ups, no life stories before the recipe. Just tested, seasonal cooking with a focus on heritage techniques, foraged ingredients, and the quiet joy of feeding people.

“Good food is mostly about paying attention.”

— Maren Ellsworth

Our approach

We believe that cooking is less about perfection and more about presence. A braise that simmers for hours, a loaf of bread that rises overnight, a jar of preserved lemons left to cure on the counter — these are small acts of patience that reward us with deep flavor and a sense of calm.

Every recipe on this site has been cooked in a home kitchen, tested with real ingredients, and written for the way people actually cook: with what's in season, what's in the pantry, and what time allows.

Meet the author

Writer, cook, forager

Maren Ellsworth

Maren is a cook, writer, and forager based in the Pacific Northwest. She has spent the last decade working in farm kitchens, writing about food, and teaching sourdough workshops.

Her work is rooted in the belief that the best meals are often the simplest ones — a pot of beans, a roasted chicken, a cake made with olive oil and rosemary.

Questions from the kitchen

Do I need special equipment for slow cooking?
A heavy-bottomed pot (enameled cast iron or stainless steel) and a tight-fitting lid are really all you need. A Dutch oven is ideal, but any sturdy pot will do. A slow cooker works too, though browning on the stovetop first makes a big difference.
How do I know if my sourdough starter is ready?
It should double in size within 4–6 hours of feeding, smell pleasantly yeasty (not acetone-like), and be full of bubbles. If it's sluggish, keep feeding it consistently at room temperature for a few more days.
Can I reduce salt in preserved lemons?
Salt is the preservative, so reducing it significantly shortens shelf life. For a low-salt version, use the refrigerator method and consume within 3–4 weeks. The saltiness also mellows as they cure.
What's the best way to store fresh herbs?
Treat them like flowers: trim the stems, place in a jar with an inch of water, cover loosely with a plastic bag, and refrigerate. Hardy herbs like rosemary and thyme can be wrapped in a damp towel.
